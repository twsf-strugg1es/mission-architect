Mission Architect: Video A (local judging, under 4 minutes)
Save the recording as voice-a.mp3 (or .m4a or .wav) in tools/video/assets/.
Read at a calm, normal speed. The video fills the time with gameplay.

How to record
- One file for this video. A quiet room. Phone about 20 cm from your mouth.
- Read each line word for word. Do not read the line numbers.
- Pause about 2 seconds after each line.
- If you make a mistake, pause 2 seconds, then read the whole line again.
- Say OSDR as "O-S-D-R", DONKI as "DON-kee", JPL as "J-P-L", Bennu as "BEN-oo", delta-v as "delta vee".

1. We are team Minus Zero: Ahmad Farruk Towsif and MD Toslim Uddin Rahat. Our project is Mission Architect, built for the Space Mission Design Game challenge.

2. Real space missions take years and huge teams to design, so students never get to feel how mission design actually works — how one choice about fuel, power or money decides whether a mission lives or dies.

3. Mission Architect turns that into a game anyone can play in a browser.

4. You pick a destination — the Moon, Mars, or asteroid Bennu — choose a real rocket and engine, and build your spacecraft part by part.

5. A real rocket equation checks your delta-v live.

6. Too heavy, no power, not enough fuel — the game will not let you launch, and it tells you why in plain language.

7. In flight, real dangers strike: solar flares, dust storms, equipment failures. Whether you survive depends on what you built.

8. Every mission ends with a grade and a debrief that explains your own decisions.

9. It runs in any browser, on a phone or a computer, by mouse or keyboard.

10. Our game runs on real NASA data: the NASA Open Science Data Repository, including radiation data; NASA Planetary Fact Sheets for distance, gravity and delta-v; the NASA JPL Small-Body Database for asteroid Bennu; and NASA DONKI space-weather data behind our solar flare events.

11. Mission Architect — design it, build it, fly it. Play it live — the link is on our project page.
